Your 8-week roadmap

Lessons

1Know your guitar

Headstock & tuning pegs at the top: turning them tightens or loosens strings. The nut is the small strip where strings leave the headstock. The frets are the metal wires along the neck. The body has the sound hole and the bridge.

Strings are numbered 6 → 1: string 6 is the thickest (low E), string 1 is the thinnest (high e).

Standard tuning, thick to thin: E A D G B E. Memory trick: Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie.

2Sitting & holding
  • Sit on a chair without armrests. Guitar's waist rests on your right thigh, body upright, neck pointing slightly up.
  • Right forearm rests on the top edge of the body; your strumming hand sits over the sound hole.
  • Left thumb goes behind the neck, roughly behind your middle finger. Don't squeeze the neck like a cricket bat.
  • Keep shoulders loose. If anything aches, stop and reset.
3Fretting: finger numbers & pressing

Left-hand fingers: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky. Chord diagrams use these numbers.

  • Press with the very tip of the finger, curved like a hook, so you don't touch neighbouring strings.
  • Press just behind the fret wire (towards the headstock), not on top of it and not in the middle of the gap.
  • Use the least pressure that gives a clean note. Squeezing harder isn't the fix; better placement is.
  • Keep left-hand nails short.
4Reading chord diagrams

Picture the guitar standing up and facing you. Vertical lines are strings (left = thick E, right = thin e). Horizontal lines are frets; the thick bar on top is the nut.

  • Dot with a number: put that finger there.
  • O above a string: play it open. ×: don't play it.
  • A long bar across strings means a barre: one finger flattens across several strings.
  • "2fr" next to the diagram means the diagram starts at fret 2.

Open the Chords tab, tap any chord, and hit ▶ to hear how it should sound.

5Strumming basics
  • Hold the pick between the thumb pad and the side of your index finger, with a little tip showing.
  • Strum from the wrist, like shaking water off your hand, not from the elbow.
  • Downstrokes land on the beats (1 2 3 4) and upstrokes on the "and"s (1 & 2 &).
  • Keep your hand moving down-up all the time, like a pendulum. For a "missing" strum, just miss the strings (a ghost strum). This is the secret to steady rhythm.
  • Upstrokes only need to hit the top 3–4 strings.

Hear and practise patterns in Practice → Strumming patterns.

6Smooth chord changes (muscle memory)
  • One-minute changes: switch between two chords for 60 seconds and count clean changes. Beat yesterday's number. The trainer tracks it for you.
  • Lift and land all fingers together as one shape, not one by one.
  • Pivot fingers: C ↔ Am, only the ring finger moves. Em → Am, the shape shifts one string down. G ↔ Em, the middle finger can stay near fret 2.
  • Air changes: hover the fingers 1 cm above the strings in the new shape, then land.
  • Slow is smooth: 60 bpm, change on beat 1. Speed comes on its own.
  • Never stop strumming. Keep the rhythm going even if the chord isn't fully ready. Songs forgive a muddy chord, not a stopped beat.
  • Three 10-minute sessions beat one 30-minute session.
7The capo (Bollywood's best friend)

A capo clamps across all strings at a fret and raises the pitch. You keep playing the same easy shapes, and the song comes out in the original key.

  • Place it just behind the fret, straight, firm enough that nothing buzzes.
  • Many Hindi songs are in keys like F or C#, which need barre chords. With a capo they become C, G or D shapes.
  • Re-tune quickly after putting the capo on if it pulls strings sharp.
  • Chord names in the Songs tab are the shapes you play with the capo on.
8Finger pain & calluses
  • Sore fingertips in weeks 1–3 are normal. Calluses form in about 2–4 weeks of daily play.
  • Short, frequent sessions (10–15 min) beat long ones early on.
  • Avoid playing right after a long shower; soft skin blisters easily.
  • Sharp pain, numbness or wrist pain is not normal. Stop and rest.
9Using the checker well
  • Quiet room, phone 30–60 cm from the sound hole, fan off if possible.
  • Strum check: strum once firmly and let the chord ring. The app hears which notes are present and tells you what's missing.
  • String-by-string: hold the chord and pluck one string at a time, slowly. This finds the exact string that's muted or buzzing. Apps like Yousician do the same thing.
  • Tune first. An out-of-tune guitar confuses any checker.
10What comes next
  • Full F barre: start with easy F, then Fmaj7, then the barre. Roll the index finger slightly onto its bony side.
  • Fingerpicking: thumb (P) plays strings 6–4, index (I) string 3, middle (M) string 2, ring (A) string 1. Try P-I-M-A on any chord.
  • Power chords & riffs for rock songs.
  • Strumming dynamics: soft verses, bigger choruses. This is what makes Arijit songs sound right.
